Research

I am a Postdoctoral Research Fellow in Social Psychology at the University of Michigan. My research centers on how internal states become interpersonal—how our emotions, moral judgments, and social perceptions shape the experiences and behaviors of the people around us. I am fascinated by how underlying feelings and judgments “leak out” through behaviors and bodily responses, and how social contexts—whether in collaborative teams, close relationships, or in first encounters—shape the way people manage and express these feelings.

To study these processes, I use a combination of field studies, in-lab and online dyadic interaction studies, and mobile app-based studies. Across these studies, I use psychophysiology, behavioral measures, and natural language processing to capture how these internal states manifest through subtle social cues, pursuing questions like: How do high- and low-status team members work together under stress? How do teams work differently with newcomers than with established team members? Can our emotions unintentionally “leak out” and affect our interactions with strangers, and when are our perceptions influenced by others’ emotions?

In my current work, I’ve been particularly interested in the influence of moral emotions on our connections with others, to understand how morality and politics impact relationships.
